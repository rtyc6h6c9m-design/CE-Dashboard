What this is

An interactive map of publicly funded circular economy research in the UK. It covers — projects awarded — between 2006 and 2026, the organisations that held them, and the outputs they produced. It is the companion artefact to a dissertation, Full Circle: Mapping the UK Circular Economy Research Ecosystem Inputs and Outputs.

How the dataset was built

Projects were collected from UKRI's Gateway to Research API using an eighteen-term search vocabulary, then screened with a three-tier, literature-grounded filter derived principle-first from Kirchherr et al. (2017) and the Ellen MacArthur Foundation glossary. Screening reached precision 0.943, recall 0.909, F1 0.926 and Cohen's κ 0.839 against an independently double-coded sample.

Outputs were matched from four sources — Gateway to Research, OpenAlex, Scopus and Web of Science — by grant reference, then deduplicated into one harmonised record per outcome. Institutions were resolved against ROR. Disciplines were assigned by a classifier with out-of-fold accuracy 0.724 and macro-F1 0.663 at a confidence threshold of 0.5029.

Limitations you should read before drawing conclusions

The consortium network is not a map of all UK collaboration

Partner organisations are named to Gateway to Research by some funders and not others. EPSRC, BBSRC, NERC, AHRC and ESRC name no partners at all. Only 309 of 1,640 projects (18.8%) list any partner, and 208 of those are Innovate UK. The consortium network is therefore a map of collaborative R&D consortia, chiefly Innovate UK and ISCF, and is dominated by companies for that reason and not because UK circular economy research is industry-led. The co-authorship network is the complementary view of the academic half.

Reported output is not found output

Innovate UK reports zero outputs to Gateway to Research across 612 projects and £125.3M, yet 845 outputs are findable across the four sources and 170 of those projects have a publication. Every output figure here is the all-source merged count, not the GtR-reported count; using the latter would overstate the no-output rate by 284 projects.

Other things to hold in mind

  • Award values. 21% of projects have no usable award value, mostly studentships. Funding figures use the 1,294 projects that do.
  • Named investigators. A principal investigator is recorded for only 385 of 1,640 projects (23.5%), in surname-plus-initial form, which cannot reliably tell two people with the same surname and initial apart. Names are shown on the project record where present; they are deliberately not searchable or networked here.
  • Geography. Lead organisation country resolves for about half of projects, because ROR does not cover small UK companies. Any geographic reading describes the academic and public half of the ecosystem.
  • Citations. Counts shown are OpenAlex only and are missing for many publications. Scopus and Web of Science counts are used in the dissertation but are proprietary and excluded here.
  • Time. Year is present for 7,501 publications; roughly 2,100 drop out of any time series. Elapsed time is measured to a fixed reference date of 6 August 2026.
  • Co-authorship. Edges require at least two co-authored outputs. 29 papers with more than 50 authors are excluded from edge construction, since hyperauthorship would otherwise generate hundreds of thousands of spurious ties.

Reproducing it

The site is static: every figure is computed in the browser from JSON extracted from the project database by scripts/export_dashboard_data.py, with network layouts pre-computed by scripts/compute_layouts.py. Re-running both against a newer database rebuilds the whole dashboard.
